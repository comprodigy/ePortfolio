Professional self-assessment

I came into the Computer Science program wanting to build applications and stayed with that plan. Eight years of part-time coursework turned “developer” into something more specific: application development with a mobile and backend lean, and a real data and security foundation. The ePortfolio is where that shows up as work, not a claim on a resume.

Coursework outside the three artifacts shaped that direction. Design classes taught me to move from requirements to modular code instead of starting in the IDE. Security courses pushed “everything is invalid until it is not,” which is why plaintext passwords and open write routes stand out to me now. Testing taught me that a green test that never calls the method is not a test. Those habits are why the enhancements look the way they do.

The five outcomes are the job skills I am claiming. Collaboration and decision support show up as data a non-developer can use. Professional communication is this site, the code review video, and the narratives. Data structures and algorithms are a deliberate choice of structure and a stated trade-off. Software engineering and databases are layered design, a repository, schema, indexes, and tools used the way a team would. Security is hashed credentials, sessions, validation, and least-privilege writes.

The three artifacts sit under that. CompuStocker is the software design piece: a CS 360 inventory app restructured so the Activity is not the application. The Contact service is the algorithms piece: a CS 320 store that already used a HashMap, tightened so unique IDs are checked against the live map. Travlr is the database piece: a CS 465 MEAN app whose unfinished auth is now wired, with a resort report for staff. Together they show the same habit: take a working class project, name the real gap, and fix that gap instead of adding a screen.

I am targeting junior backend or mobile roles. Employers in that lane expect clean structure, tests, efficient data access, and not storing secrets in plaintext. This portfolio is the evidence for those interviews.
